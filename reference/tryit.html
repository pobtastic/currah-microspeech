Currah μSpeech Try s$

Type a Currah speech string as you would assign to s$ and hear the SP0256-AL2 allophones that ParseSpeechString would queue. This is not English text-to-speech: letters map one-for-one, and names in parentheses pick a specific sound.

Ctrl+Enter also speaks.

A full stop queues two PA5 pauses (the longest). A space is a word pause, a comma is a phrase pause (one PA5), and an apostrophe is a short pause. Uppercase raises the pitch by about 1.07×. Q and X are not letters in this alphabet; write "kw" and "ks" instead.

The parser follows ParseSpeechString and ParseAllophoneGroup. Samples are recordings of a real AL2 chip, concatenated in the browser; they are close to the unit, not a cycle-accurate SP0256 core. The samples derive from CPCWiki's AL2 WAV set (via ExtremeElectronics), which CPCWiki notes has some allophone lengths wrong.
